Day 2 — Ada grows hands

2026-10-15

What I did

  • Instrumented the hands, overlay by overlay, until every WebXR hand-tracking layer was live on both hands: white joint markers, wireframe radius spheres (each joint's reported size), orientation lines, and aiming rays.
  • Added a cyan ray out of the index finger, derived from the tracked finger joints. "Perfect. This is working perfectly." Hands prototyping for this stage: done.
  • Saved the combination as my first developer preset. It lives in project memory for now — "we have to do better than project memory, but we'll fix that later."
  • Identified the three mystery panels: the room panel, the capability catalog, and a browser-sharing panel that pipes a Mac Chrome tab into the headset — video, audio and input. That test comes next.
  • Asked for the dev-only menus to collapse into a single "developer settings" panel instead of cluttering the normal view.
  • Handed a speech bug to a sub-agent: Ada answered a sentence I never said. "I didn't say any of that. Let's book that as a bug."

Why / what I learned

  • Hands are how a room becomes an interface — if I can point at a surface and have Ada know exactly what I mean, the whole space becomes addressable.
  • Watching the raw tracking data while building against it is the whole trick: you can't debug an interaction you can't see.
  • Found a real one: long builds stop when the headset comes off. Not broken — just waiting — but work has to survive the headset leaving the head.

Next

  • Browser panel test: a Mac Chrome tab inside the headset — video and audio first, then clicking and scrolling.
  • Figure out why builds stall when the headset comes off.
