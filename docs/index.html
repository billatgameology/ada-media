An open experiment · Development journal

Ada

Rethinking how we interact with computers.

An open exploration of spatial computing, AI, and human-computer interaction.

What if working with computers felt less like operating software and more like collaborating with another presence? This is Ada's development journal — experiments, breakthroughs, failures, and everything in between.

  1. Sunday, October 25, 2026

    The demo is the test

    • The demo runs end to end: Ada loads the 30mm holder, sends a print sub-agent, opens the YC application, writes and saves the description, fills the tech-stack section from a capture, and does not submit.
    • The object becomes a print: the 20mm holder is too shallow, so the 30mm design gets a center divider, exports to STL, and reaches the printer in white PLA with a three-hour estimate.
    • The seams show under pressure: contact-grab jitter, ambiguity and conflict guards rejecting valid loads and resizes, and stale worker cards force restarts before the final take.

    Why it matters The demo is the integration test. Features that survive a live run with a camera and a person waiting are the ones that count.

  2. Saturday, October 24, 2026

    The object should remember

    • The whiteboard holds: all five media items move flush, video pickup passes, and hover-arm plus the two-curl summon work, though the gesture is still inconsistent.
    • The cube becomes transparent: solid edges, see-through faces, and contact, hover and distance grab; the preview style becomes the default after 2,382 tests and 84 native checks.
    • Selection does not survive the next turn: the app knows the Z-face and the 50mm extension, but rejects the resize because the face was not selected in the new message.

    Why it matters A collaborator should carry the thread of a task. If every follow-up command has to re-prove its target, the conversation turns into a form.

  3. Friday, October 23, 2026

    Cyan means she saw you

    • The whiteboard click: all five media items land flat and stay fixed to the board; the evening test confirms the fix.
    • Cyan feedback: a raised index finger or an open palm turns the hand outline cyan, then back to white when the pose ends.
    • The summon window is too short: the hover cue gives about two seconds to beckon, the curl does not reliably count, and the progress bar covers the media.

    Why it matters Recognition needs a visible yes before it asks for the next gesture. The cyan outline is the handshake.

  4. Thursday, October 22, 2026

    All five, but not flat

    • One request, five objects: Ada moved the photo and four videos to the whiteboard in one go, but they kept their old viewing angles and stuck out of the board.
    • Multi-select is a foundation, not a feature: select one or more objects, say one command, and reuse the same move, resize and hide actions for the set.
    • The summon gesture takes shape: hover to arm, then curl and reopen four fingers twice; the object arrives near your hand, unheld. Video playback and two-hand resize pass.

    Why it matters The interesting problem is not one group-move command. It is an object model that can select a set, preserve each item’s relationship to a surface, and accept the same verbs in a group.

  5. Wednesday, October 21, 2026

    The message was kept

    • Media, minus the chrome: the photo/video library drops its backing frames, persistent filenames and giant header; play/pause and labels appear only on hover or grab.
    • Failure gets a row: a rejected request stays visible as “failed, message kept” instead of disappearing from the queue; remove and resend are the next controls.
    • Finished is not closed: seven finished workers still counted as open, and voice-moving the screen mirror or dialogue history still needs work — the mirror found the ceiling.

    Why it matters A trustworthy assistant has to make failure legible. The work that mattered today was keeping the failed request, the unclosed worker and the bad move visible enough to repair.

  6. Monday, October 19, 2026

    Ada drives Chrome from inside the headset

    • From VR, Ada switches Chrome tabs on the Mac, fills the YC draft and verifies the form after reload without submitting.
    • Wireless ADB reconnects by itself at startup, with one bounded attempt and a fresh headset identity check.
    • Named movement works — “move Ada to the whiteboard” — while pointing moves fail with an honest explanation instead of silence.

    Why it matters Working together starts to mean using the same tools: I can talk through a task from the headset while Ada operates Chrome on the Mac, with the final submission still in my hands.

  7. Sunday, October 18, 2026

    Ada gets eyes

    • Ada gets eyes: a fresh Quest capture of the real room, including passthrough and virtual content, in a movable photo panel.
    • Vision permission and wireless ADB become separate, measured states that expire when the connection goes stale.
    • Mac screen sharing becomes a standalone, billboarded frame with move, resize, close and independent near/far interaction.

    Why it matters A fresh headset capture gives Ada visual context from the room I'm in, so our conversation can refer to what I'm seeing as well as what I'm saying.

  8. Saturday, October 17, 2026

    Keep listening, and Ada learns to move

    • Keep listening: holding a raised index finger through a pause stops Ada cutting speech off.
    • Ada relocates on command — “move to the server rack” glides her there with the ducklings following.
    • The message queue is now visible and editable, and deletion no longer requires an exact magic phrase.
  9. Friday, October 16, 2026

    The room becomes editable

    • The room becomes editable: select, rename, relabel and delete surfaces. Revision 5 kept 5 labels and removed 8 surfaces.
    • Worker cubes appear beside Ada as real background tasks, driven by observations rather than animation.
    • Speech and chat finally match, streaming updates as they arrive instead of one block at the end.
  10. Thursday, October 15, 2026

    Ada grows hands

    • All WebXR hand overlays at once: joint markers, radius spheres, orientation axes and aim rays, plus a cyan index-finger ray.
    • The first developer preset saves the hand view to project memory; selectable presets are still missing.
    • The browser panel lands in code — Mac Chrome inside XR with video, audio and input — though the first live test still ends with an interruption.

    Why it matters Hands make the room addressable: pointing at a surface is a step toward sharing what I mean with Ada, without translating everything into words.

  11. Wednesday, October 14, 2026

    Ada, take four

    • First working room workspace: import the scanned room, align it, place a persistent anchor and save the layout.
    • A full reload restores the same room, with up to 64 named surfaces and 50 spatial notes.
    • Fresh-only capture: historical replay omitted, payloads capped at 48 KB, secrets redacted and the private outbox enforced.
