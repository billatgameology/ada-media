Day 5 — Ada gets eyes

2026-10-18

What I did

  • Gave Ada eyes: Quest screenshots now land in a movable photo panel inside the app. She read my equipment rack back from a fresh capture — Dell monitor, drive bays, green LEDs, cables. "It worked!"
  • Added a status window: vision permission and wireless ADB as separate red/green lines, checked every few seconds, with stale results expiring. Now I can see at a glance whether "go to the whiteboard" is actually possible.
  • Gave the photo panel real UI: detached from the vision menu, billboards toward my head, near-hand grab, two-hand resize (out = bigger, in = smaller), red close box — distance grab is next.
  • Started the screen-mirroring rework: the stream becomes a standalone billboarded view that stays where I put it, with its own stop control; the settings menu can move or close without killing it.
  • Movement bug batch: found why released ducklings snap into a line (a settling step returns them to fixed resting slots), why a drag cancels and snaps back (a change in the other hand cancels it), and caught combine breaking with the grid on — plus queue scrolling past three messages and palm targets still dropping.
  • Fixed failure communication: an action gate was suppressing the whole final reply when a move failed, honest explanation included. Now a blocked action still tells me why. 1,547 tests, staged for the headset.

Why / what I learned

  • "Can you see this?" becomes a real question once screenshots work. The status lights mean I don't have to guess whether the headset is connected before asking for something.
  • Most of the day was the unglamorous middle: capture mismatches, snapback paths, stale status. Failure paths deserve the same care as features — especially when the assistant is supposed to be trustworthy.

Next

  • Exact palm targeting, drag-release retention, finished-cube cleanup, combine with grid on, and the first live screen-stream test.
